Case Study — Healthcare AI · Staff Product Designer

Clinico AI — Intelligent
Care Copilot System

Staff Product Designer
14 months
Web SaaS + Mobile

Overview

Clinico AI is a continuous intelligence layer between patients and doctors — built to prevent the silent failures that happen between appointments. The system surfaces the right signals to the right user at the right time, serving two fundamentally different audiences: clinicians managing caseloads and patients managing their own health.

Problem

A cardiologist managing 80 patients with real-time push alerts for every metric shift would be non-functional within a day. Patients shown raw lab values like "BNP 287, eGFR 64" without context experienced anxiety, not clarity. The challenge was designing AI that translates clinical reality into actionable signals — for both sides.

Research Insights

Clinicians needed signal prioritisation, not raw data — alert fatigue was a critical safety issue
Patients understood "Medication gap detected" far better than "metoprolol adherence: 34%"
The interruption cost of a false-positive alert during surgery or a critical consultation is not zero — it's dangerous
Trust in AI recommendations collapsed after 3–4 bad experiences with inconsistent outputs

User Personas

👩‍⚕️
Dr. Anand, 44
Cardiologist, 80-patient caseload
"I don't need more data. I need to know which patient needs me today."
🧓
Rajan, 62
Patient with chronic condition
"The numbers scare me. Tell me what I should actually do."

Design Process

01 Discover Understand the Healthcare Problem.
02 Define Identify high-impact problems.
03 AI Opportunity Find where AI can help.
04 Structure Create clear user journeys.
05 Design Translate ideas into interfaces.
06 Prototype Bring interactions to life.
07 Validate Test and iterate.
08 Refine Balance intelligence with trust.

Key Design Decisions

Clinician layer: severity-ranked alerts with AI confidence scores, not chronological feeds
Patient layer: plain-language summaries with one clear action, not lab value dumps
Transparency layer: AI shows its reasoning so clinicians can override with context

Results

11min
Time saved per patient review
78%
Alert accuracy rate
91%
Patient plan adherence

Case Study — Fintech UX · Mobile

QuickCash —
Cardless cash.

UX / UI Designer
Product UX · Interaction · UI
Mobile (Android + iOS)

Overview

QuickCash explores a simple question: if people already use UPI on their phones every day, can getting physical cash be just as effortless? The concept connects the user's phone, a nearby ATM, and a secure UPI payment flow — intentionally broken into clear, focused steps so the user always knows what happens next.

Problem

Traditional ATM withdrawal assumes a physical debit card. QuickCash removes that dependency entirely. The design challenge: create a cardless withdrawal flow that feels familiar, secure, and fast — while bridging a digital UPI interaction with a physical ATM machine.

User Flow

Home → ATM Locator → QR Scanner → Withdraw Amount → UPI PIN → Processing → Success / Failed → History
Each screen represents one decision or system state — this reduces cognitive load and makes error recovery easier to design

Research Insights

Time pressure at ATMs creates anxiety — the entire flow needed to be completable in under 30 seconds
OTP-based confirmations were the most trusted security pattern among users
Users expected clear session expiry feedback to avoid failed withdrawals
Success and failure states needed to be unambiguous — money is involved, stakes are high

User Personas

🧑‍🎓
Rahul, 22
College student, UPI-first user
"I never carry my card but I need cash sometimes — this should just work."
👩‍💼
Meera, 38
Professional, frequent traveller
"I left my wallet at home again — I need a reliable backup fast."

Key UI Screens

Home
ATM Locator
UPI PIN + Success / Failure
Transaction History

Reflection

"The strongest part of the concept is not removing the card — it is making every transition feel intentional enough that the user trusts what is happening to their money."

Case Study — AI EdTech · School Intelligence Platform

EduPulse AI —
Schools have data.
They've never had intelligence.

Lead UX / AI Experience Designer
Web SaaS + Tablet
Teachers · Admins · Parents · Students

Overview

EduPulse AI connects student information, learning signals, and wellbeing data into a single living system — so schools can act before students fall through the cracks. Unlike traditional SIS platforms, EduPulse surfaces intelligence, not just data.

Problem

Schools were drowning in data but starved of insight. A teacher and an admin have fundamentally different cognitive contexts — a unified dashboard forces uncomfortable compromises: too much detail for the admin, too little for the teacher. Role-specific surfaces let us design for actual mental models, not theoretical ones.

Research Insights

Teachers averaged 4.8 context switches per hour across disconnected tools
At-risk students were identified weeks too late because signals were siloed across systems
The feedback loop: teacher action feeds back into the AI model — improving future signal accuracy over time
Wellbeing data required extreme sensitivity in UI — stigma reduction was a design requirement, not a nice-to-have

User Personas

👩‍🏫
Divya, 34
Class Teacher, 120 students
"I can tell something is off with a student but I have no data to act on."
🧑‍💼
Suresh, 48
Principal / School Admin
"I'm seeing outputs, not signals. I can't get ahead of problems."

Design Process

01 Discover Understand the education ecosystem.
02 Define Identify workflow friction.
03 Prioritize Focus on meaningful AI opportunities.
04 Structure Build the information architecture.
05 Explore Test different interaction models.
06 Design Create the product experience.
07 Systemize Build scalable UI patterns.
08 Iterate Prototype, validate, and refine.

Case Study — AI Interaction Design · System-Level UX

Designing AI Systems —
Prompt Libraries &
Interaction Patterns

System-Level AI UX Design
Medical · Finance · Education · Wellness
AI Interaction · Prompt Engineering · UX Strategy

Thesis

"AI products succeed or fail based on interaction design and prompt systems — not just model capability."

Overview

A system-level design project examining how prompt architecture, interaction patterns, and behavior modeling determine whether AI products succeed — across four high-stakes domains. The output is a reusable framework, not a single product.

Domain Patterns

Medical: Symptom-aware · Escalation-mapped · Safety-first prompting
Finance: Confidence-calibrated · Audit-transparent · Risk-disclosed responses
Education: Scaffolded · Hint-first · Mastery-focused learning interactions
Wellness: Emotion-aware · Habit-focused · Non-prescriptive support patterns

Research Insights

AI trust collapses after 3–4 bad experiences with inconsistent or wrong outputs
Users interpret confident-sounding AI responses as factual — tone design is a safety issue
The same prompt produces different results on different days — behavioral consistency is a UX problem

The Prompt Architecture

A. System Prompts Establish the AI's identity, tone, constraints, and domain specialization. Written by designers and engineers. Updated deliberately, not per-session.
B. User Prompts Raw natural language from the user. Highly variable in clarity, intent, and emotional state. Must be interpreted, not just forwarded.
C. Context Prompts User history, preferences, behavioral signals, and goals. Injected by the system into each request. The primary mechanism for personalization.
D. Dynamic Prompts Real-time modifiers that change the AI's behavior based on detected user state — fatigue, urgency, emotional distress, learning pace, or financial risk level.

Impact

+34%
Task success rate with framework
5×
Prompt reusability across domains
+28%
User trust score improvement
